# Patron dialogue — the death ladder

*Generated 2026-08-12 by six per-patron writing agents, then curated. Mechanic is
`23-THE-PATH-SYSTEM.md` §9.1b.*

> **These are CANDIDATES, not canon.** The standing tone ruling
> (`18-THE-STALKERS.md`) is that the patrons' words are **Ethan's own writing**.
> Everything below is a draft for him to take, cut or rewrite. Where a line of his
> already exists it is marked CANON and outranks anything here.

## How to read this

One pick per beat, chosen from three candidates each. The seven beats:

| beat | when |
|---|---|
| 1 FIRST BLOOD | one death. barely remarked on |
| 2 A PATTERN | several. the patron has noticed |
| 3 ESCALATION | this is becoming a problem — or, for three of them, a pleasure |
| 4 THE WARNING | what happens next, stated |
| 5 FORCED HARVEST | the patron comes early |
| 6 DISMISSAL | thrown off the path |
| COOLING | the counter decays. they notice |

**The counter is one number with six readings** (§9.1b). Blade reads contempt,
Forge debt, Crown embarrassment — but **Wall reads grief, Salvage opportunity,
and Art readiness.** Those three escalate the wrong way on purpose.

## ⭐ BLADE — The Challenger
**Best of the six.** Matches the canon register without imitating it: two- and
three-word lines, contempt, no explanation, the allusion varied off Icarus rather
than repeated. Ship with light trimming.

| beat | line |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Already.** |
| 2 | **You mistake struggle for growth.** |
| 3 | **You waste what sustains me.** |
| 4 | **Stop. Before I decide for you.** |
| 5 | **As Phaethon fell, so do you.** |
| 6 | **What I needed is mine. Go.** |
| COOL | **You falter less. For now.** |

Alternates worth keeping: "Clumsy." · "Again." · "I see the shape of your ending."
· "This grows tedious." · "Your thread snaps. I arrive."

CANON, unchanged: *"Fall"* · *"You reach for heights you will never attain"* ·
*"For It was Icarus who flew too close to the sun. You will share his fate"* ·
*"Run."* — `"Run."` stays reserved for the beat before a real Harvest.

## ⭐ ART — The Nightmare
**Second best, and the strangest.** The inverted ladder landed: she gets happier
as you die. Her cooling line is the strongest single cooling line in the document,
because patient disappointment beats grudging approval.

| beat | line |
|---|---|
| 1 | **There. You closed your eyes. Good. So good.** |
| 2 | **You fall and you fall and you fall. It pleases me so.** |
| 3 | **How heavy your eyes must be. How heavy. Close them now.** |
| 4 | **I will gather you soon. You will rest. Rest perfectly. It approaches.** |
| 5 | **Let me in. Let me take. You're ready. So very ready. Sleep.** |
| 6 | **You woke too soon. How sad. I will wait. Wait for you.** |
| COOL | **You've forgotten to rest. I remember. I remember for you. I wait.** |

⚠️ "gather" carries beats 4 and 5 and also collides with the Harvest's own
vocabulary. Vary one of them.
Cut: *"The pages have spoken. I speak now."* — clunky, and it gestures at a
mechanic.

## ⭐ WALL — The Mother
**Excellent, one line must go.** Grief rather than anger throughout, exactly as
briefed.

| beat | line |
|---|---|
| 1 | **You fell. I felt it. Come back to us where it's safe.** |
| 2 | **You're leaving us over and over. Are you searching? Or just... leaving?** |
| 3 | **Do you understand what this does? Every death takes a little more of me.** |
| 4 | **We can't keep doing this. You leave, I break. So I'm bringing you home now.** |
| 5 | **This is mercy, darling. This is me keeping my promise. I won't lose you again.** |
| 6 | **I'll live in the walls you build. Even when you don't see me. Especially then.** |
| COOL | **I can feel you trying. It means so much. Just... stay close to us now.** |

🚨 **CUT:** *"But I'm running out of patience now."* — she never threatens and
never loses patience. That is Blade's register leaking into hers, and it is
precisely the thing that would make her ordinary.

**Beat 6 is the best single line in this document.** The dismissal as heartbreak,
and a threat that never admits to being one — she retreats *into the walls the
Wall player builds*, which is her whole character in one sentence.

**OPEN:** she uses **"love"** and **"darling"**. No other patron uses pet names.
That is a real register choice and it is Ethan's to make.

## CROWN — The False King

a death is embarrassing to **him**.

| beat | line |
|---|---|
| 1 | **You were not sent to die. This lapse is noted.** |
| 2 | **You die where you should only command.** |
| 3 | **My judgment in selecting you grows questionable. Disabuse me of this doubt.** |

| COOL | **Ah. You remember your station at last. How adequate.** |

Also strong: *"Kings do not shed their blood in the dirt. Why were you exposed?"*

that he keeps one.

⚠️ **BEAT 5 NEEDS A PASS.** "Your time has expired" / "The reckoning arrives" is

collection as an administrative formality he is slightly bored by.

## FORGE — The Thief
**Weakest of the six. Needs a full rewrite pass.** The agent drifted into
corporate finance vocabulary despite the brief warning against exactly that.
"Hemorrhaging productivity", "a poor investment", "debts settle slowly" is an
accountant, not a horned goat-demon furious about lost output.

Only two survive intact:

| beat | line |
|---|---|
| 3 | **Every death is a factory unfinished, a machine that rusts away.** |
| 6 | **Go cool in the dark. When you remember how to build, return.** |

⚠️ **REWRITE THE REST.** The target is **petty and menacing at once** — he grieves
the WORK and never the worker, and the joke is that a demon this ancient is this
annoyed about a delivery being late. Strip the MBA register; keep "late", "owed",
"idle", "wasted"; lose "interest", "assets", "investment", "productivity".

# PART II — THE AMBIENT LAYER

*Second pass, 2026-08-12. Ethan: "sensory sounds, chain rattling, irritation,
animal noises, etc to add more fluff to the patron's irritation."*

**A different register from Part I.** These are not spoken. They are things the
player NOTICES — third person, present tense, in asterisks. The existing whispers
in `stalker.js` are already this voice, and are Ethan's own:

> *The goat whispers in a distinctly southern accent you don't get the reference*
> *The spider attempts to have a tea party. The tea is all over the floor*
> *The wolf is an alcoholic. You are in danger*
> *AWOOOO*

Atmospheric and absurd at once — deliberately not pure horror. Four tiers,
escalating on the same counter as Part I.

## 🚨 IMPLEMENTATION CONSEQUENCE — anchored lines, not just random pools

The Art agent produced the best structural idea in either pass. It took Ethan's
own canon line and made it a **mutating refrain across all four tiers**:

| tier | line |
|---|---|
| 1 | *She wants you to sleep. She wants you to **rest**.* |
| 2 | *She wants you to sleep. She wants you **safe**.* |
| 3 | *She wants you to sleep now. She wants you **warm**.* |
| 4 | *She wants you to sleep **forever**. She wants you **here**.* |

The first half barely moves; the second drifts from *rest* to *here*; "forever"
appears only at the end.

This was going to be built as **random picks from a per-tier pool**. It must also
support **ANCHORED lines** — one per patron, recurring at every tier in an evolving
form, so the player recognises it and hears it change. The random pool stays for
everything else. **The design came out of the writing.**

Wall found the same trick independently, running one motif across three tiers:
T1 *The house breathes when you do.* → T2 *The breathing has a rhythm now. It
matches yours.* → T4 *Her breath mingles with yours.*

## CROWN — procession, protocol, a ledger

⚠️ **Crown refused the comedy brief in BOTH passes.** Five patrons take to the
absurdist register happily; he will not, twice, under two different briefs.

**Recommendation: let him be the straight man.** A court of the dead taking itself
with total seriousness, in a world where a goat is cursing in an impossible
register, is funnier than making him tell jokes. One humourless patron out of six
is a feature, not a gap.

| tier | lines |
|---|---|
| 1 | *Something is being written down. Far away. Carefully.* · *Quills scratch against parchment. Someone keeps count.* |
| 2 | *The procession has changed course. It will not go around.* · *Robes on stone. Bone on bone. The sound is patient.* · *Your actions are being noted. Everything is being noted.* |
| 3 | *The ledger is being checked. Your name appears often.* · *The robed figure does not hurry. Patience is protocol.* |
| 4 | *The ledger closes. The verdict is written.* · *Each footfall marks a syllable in judgment.* · *Everything you have done is now accounted for.* |

## ART — pages, warmth, quiet

**Best set of the twelve.** Her ladder runs the wrong way: nothing gets louder or
closer, everything gets *softer*. The horror is that it is all becoming nicer.

| tier | lines |
|---|---|
| 1 | *Pages turn where no book exists, over and over* · *The air is warmer than it was a moment ago* · *A breath not your own, slightly out of step with yours* |
| 2 | *Pages turn behind you. You turn around. There are no pages.* · *Everything has become very quiet. Exactly how quiet, you cannot say.* · *Your pillow was not this soft when you arrived* |
| 3 | *You are so tired. When did you become so tired?* · *The blankets are tucking themselves around you. This is fine.* |
| 4 | *Your eyelids are so heavy. She has made them kind.* · *Everything smells like home. You have never been here.* · *Being tucked in. Being held. Being found at last.* |

Plus the anchored refrain above, which runs all four tiers.

## What both passes taught

1. **The stranger the constraint, the better the output.** Art (pleased by death),
   Wall (never allowed to threaten) and Blade (never allowed to explain) produced
   the best writing in both passes. Forge — a conventional angry creditor — was the
   worst at dialogue.
2. **Give an agent one trait and it treats it as a quota.** Forge's dialogue
   drowned in finance vocabulary; Salvage's ambient used a commerce simile seven
   times. Both briefs named the trait; both agents applied it to every line. The
   next brief must say *"use this vocabulary at most twice across the set."*
3. **A character can be strong in one register and weak in another.** Forge failed
   in first person and succeeded in third. Rewrite his dialogue toward pettiness
   rather than menace — that is what worked when observed.
4. **Let a refusal become a design decision.** Crown would not be funny, twice.
   That is the character asserting itself; make him the straight man rather than
   fighting it.
