# The six introductions — final text

*Pass 2, 2026-08-12, Sonnet, one agent per patron, briefed from
`27-THE-SIX-VOICES.md`. **This supersedes the scenes in `26`**, which are kept
there as the first draft. Mechanic and build plan stay in `26`.*

> **CANDIDATES.** The patrons' words are Ethan's. Curation notes are marked; the
> ✂️ items were changes to make before building, and are now **applied** — this
> file is the CURRENT TEXT, not a proposal.

**Scene shape:** black screen, rooted, ~2.5s between lines, ≈25–35s total.
**Every scene carries a false shared history and a distinct manipulation
technique** (`27` §MANIPULATION AXIS).

---

## ✅ THE ONE CROSS-CUTTING FIX *(applied)*

**Three gift lines converged on the same move** — applying the item's
can't-be-lost property to the *player*:

| | |
|---|---|
| Wall | *"it will never leave you either. Not truly. Not for long."* |
| Forge | *"Neither, from this moment, will you."* |
| Art | *"it always comes home. Like you do."* |

Three writers found it independently, which is why it is good — and why it is now
a house tic. **Keep ONE.** Wall's is the strongest fit (possession is her entire
character); Forge's is the funniest; Art's is the gentlest. Blade and Crown avoided
it and need no change.

## BLADE — The Challenger
*false history: **you already reached for it** · technique: **contempt as bait***

**ARRIVAL**
1. You already reached for it.
2. I felt the reach before you understood you had made it.
3. Stand still. You have already begun to disappoint me.
4. Phaethon reached for the sun's chariot too. They still find pieces of him in the river.
5. Hundreds have stood where you stand now. I remember none of their names.
6. I have knelt before nothing since the day I rose from it.

**DEMAND**
1. I do not want your devotion. I want to see if you are worth the trouble of watching.
2. Everything you carry, you carry because I allow it.
3. Choose. I am already losing interest.

**OPTIONS** — `Close your hand.` / `Pull it back.`

**ON ACCEPTANCE**
1. Good.
2. Everything you carried in — hand it over. It was borrowed, not earned.
3. There. Lighter. You will not miss what was never truly yours.

**ON REFUSAL**
1. Of course.
2. Better men than you have refused me. I have forgotten every one.
3. Go.

**THE GIFT** *(Dark Warblade — two-handed, dark metal, deliberately too heavy)*
1. Take it. Two hands. Even that will not be enough.
2. It will not break, and it will not stay lost — lose it in a river and it finds your hand again by the second sunrise. I did not forge it to keep you alive. I forged it to still be standing when you are not.

> ⭐ **THE GIFT IS A WEAPON YOU ARE NOT STRONG ENOUGH FOR.**
> Ethan: *"it shouldn't be oh here's a sword. No it should be here's a sword that
> feels wrong to use."* `born_in_chaos_v1:darkwarblade` was already that item before
> we touched it, and it is better than the netherite zweihander on every axis he
> named:
> * **It is from Blade's own mod.** He is `born_in_chaos_v1:fallen_chaos_knight`; he
>   hands you a blade made of the same stuff he is.
> * **`"two_handed": true`** — the line *"Take it. Two hands."* stops being flavour
>   and becomes the item's actual attribute. Better Combat is installed and shipped,
>   so the two-handed pose, the claymore swing and the 3-block reach all really play.
> * **The drawback is the mod author's, not ours.** Its tooltip warns in the mod's own
>   red: *"This weapon is excessively heavy, making it difficult to use without a
>   strength effect."* We did not bolt a cost onto a good sword. We found the sword
>   whose cost was already written.
> * **The internal model is literally named `greatdarksword`.**
>
> The reason it is the right gift is character, not stats. Blade's whole thesis is
> that you are not equal to what you reached for — and he hands you the proof, then
> says nothing about it. **He must never warn you it is heavy.** You find out mid-swing.
> *"Even that will not be enough"* was written before the item was chosen and is now
> a much better line than it was.
> Overcoming it means getting Strength — arguing with him, which is the only
> conversation he accepts.

> 🚨 **`"Run."` IS CANON-RESERVED** and must be cut here. It fires **once**,
> immediately before a real Harvest. Spending it on a declined introduction burns
> the only line marked non-repeatable.
> **Both options continue the false premise** — your hand is already out, and
> neither choice lets you say it never was. That is the technique working.
> Agent note: keep refusal delivery *identical* to acceptance. Any sting reads as
> anger and undercuts the indifference that makes refusing cost something.

## SALVAGE — The Hound
*false history: **you called me here** · technique: **the favour that is a debt***

**ARRIVAL**
1. There you are.
2. I wondered how long you'd make me wait.
3. Do not be afraid of the dark — I like to talk close, and this is close.
4. You called me, friend, and here I am, same as I always come.
5. I have watched hands reach for this life a long time before yours reached for me. Most take more than they can carry, and pay for it in pieces.
6. Let's do a deal.

**DEMAND**
1. Give me what you've grown into out here — everything you've climbed to be since you arrived. I only want to hold it.
2. It travels lighter with me than loose in your own two hands.
3. Let me hold some of it for you. I am good at holding things.

**OPTIONS** — `Let her hold it.` / `Not yet.`

**ON ACCEPTANCE**
1. Good. Just breathe — this only takes a moment, and it's mine to keep safe now, not yours to lose.
2. There. Lighter already, aren't you.
3. Now. Let's see what you can still carry.

**ON REFUSAL**
1. Mm. Not tonight, then.
2. I am not going anywhere. I never do.
3. I will still be circling.

**THE GIFT** *(sawn-off double barrel + exactly 2× 12-gauge)*
1. Here — she's yours. Two barrels, two shells, and not a scrap more than that; she was never built to carry extra.
2. Use them somewhere I can watch.

> ⭐ **`Not yet.`** — the refusal option is written in HER framing, not the
> player's. Declining is pre-labelled as a delay.
> The gift is the whole relationship in two lines: a gun that holds exactly what
> she gave you, in a world where guns cannot be crafted and shells come only from
> deep salvage or from her.
> Agent note: reveal gun first, then the two shells, so *"not a scrap more"* reads
> as description now and as a threat later.

## FORGE — The Thief
*false history: **we agreed, you have forgotten** · technique: **the assumed contract***

**ARRIVAL**
1. There you are.
2. Late — again. I've dragged this chain since before you were born, and still I'm the one who waits.
3. Sit still. This will not take long — unless you make it.
4. I was old before this dirt had a name. Did you think I'd forget an arrangement?
5. Bells ring when a debt walks by. Mine never stop.
6. You've heard that sound before. In your sleep, if nowhere else.

**DEMAND**
1. **We shook on it. Maybe not with hands — you don't recall, and that is not my concern.**
2. Everything in your pockets, and everything your pockets will ever hold. That was always the shape of it.
3. I don't care if you live to see it built. I care that it gets built. Well?

**OPTIONS** — `Fine. Take it.` / `Not tonight.`

**ON ACCEPTANCE**
1. There. See? Painless.
2. That is everything you had? Pathetic hoard — I've taken heavier tolls off men half asleep.
3. Up. There's a great deal of owing left in you yet.

**ON REFUSAL**
1. You made me wait for this?
2. I do not forget a wasted evening. I was old before memory was invented, and mine outlasts yours.
3. Fine. I'll drag this same chain past your door tomorrow, and the next, until you remember what's owed.

**THE GIFT** *(wrench)*
1. Here. Call it an advance, not a gift — I don't give things away, I lend them a longer leash.
2. It will not break. It will not stay lost.

> ⭐ **The purest gaslight line in the batch.** *"We shook on it. Maybe not with
> hands — you don't recall, and that is not my concern."* He concedes you do not
> remember and rules the concession irrelevant.
> Ran over the two-item commercial-vocabulary cap — but every term is ancient and
> personal (arrangement, toll, owing), none corporate. **The spirit held; leave it.**
> Agent note: reuse this "you're already late" voice for *every* later Forge
> delivery. A false history only holds if it is consistent past the scene that
> introduced it.

## WALL — The Mother
*false history: **you left it open for me** · technique: **the shrinking ask***

**ARRIVAL**
1. Oh. You finally turned around.
2. I have been this close the whole time, love. You never noticed.
3. Every wall you have ever set your hand to — I was already inside it, waiting for the rest of you to catch up.
4. You left a seam for me once. A gap under a door. A stone set a little loose, so something could get through.
5. I do not think you meant to leave it open. But you did, and I came through it, and I have been here since.
6. I do not mind. I am good at waiting, darling.

**DEMAND**
Let us start with something small between us.
Not much. Barely anything at all.
You will hardly know it left you.

**OPTIONS** — `Let us be closer.` / `Not this. Not now.`

**ON ACCEPTANCE**
There. Do you feel that?
Oh — I felt all of you, just then. Every bit of it.
Lighter already, aren't you, love. That is exactly how it is supposed to feel.

**ON REFUSAL**
That is alright. That is alright.
Next time I will ask for less. Just less than nothing, if that is easier for you.
I can wait that long too. I have waited longer.

**THE GIFT** *(block reinforcer)*
Here — take this. Stone forgets how to break once you have touched it with this in your hand.
Build us something worth keeping, and it will never leave you either. Not truly. Not for long.

> ⭐ **Not one threat, again.** Hardest constraint in the set, held twice.
> **The gaslight is physical, which is worse than assertion** — a seam, a gap under
> a door, a stone set a little loose. And she *grants you innocence while asserting
> the fact*: "I do not think you meant to leave it open. But you did." You cannot
> argue with someone who has already forgiven you.
> `Not this. Not now.` — even the refusal is phrased as a delay, in her voice.
> **Her gift line is the one I would KEEP** in the cross-cutting fix: possession is
> her entire character, so the move belongs to her more than to the other two.

## CROWN — The False King
*false history: **you were recommended to me** · technique: **the flattering demotion***

**ARRIVAL**
1. Be seated. You were never standing, but the correction costs nothing.
2. A court has convened. You were simply not yet worth telling.
3. **You were recommended to me, which happens rarely — I do not ask who, and it would not concern you if I did.**
4. I have counted every hour you thought yourself unwatched.
5. Others call themselves protector, guardian, friend. I keep no such pretense.
6. You were noticed before you were impressive. That is rarer, and should not be wasted.

**DEMAND**
I require an answer, not a debate. The debate concluded before you arrived.
Serve, or refuse — I have use for either word, though only one keeps your name in my count.
I am told you are decisive. I would like that to be true.

**OPTIONS** — `I will take my station.` / `I answer to no court.`

**ON ACCEPTANCE**
Good. Few answer correctly on the first true asking.
There is a toll at this gate, as at any gate worth entering — what you carried in, you do not carry in twice. Consider the account settled, not taken.
You will not miss it. A vassal is provisioned. He does not hoard.

**ON REFUSAL**
Noted, and recorded — a rare line in a very long ledger.
The court remembers those who know their own mind.
Go, then. I do not send the dead after someone who simply said no.

**THE GIFT** *(dark wand)*
Take this. It is not a weapon; it is a seal of office, and every hand that obeys you will obey it first.
Lose it if you must — it does not stay lost. The court does not misplace what it has granted.

> ⭐ **The best-engineered gaslight of the six: it is UNFALSIFIABLE BY
> CONSTRUCTION.** He pre-emptively refuses to name who recommended you, and makes
> asking beneath you both. There is no exit because he closed it before you saw it.
> *"I do not send the dead after someone who simply said no"* quietly establishes
> that he DOES send them after people who refuse some other way.
> **Straight-man ruling held completely** — not one joke in two passes. Agent note:
> deliver every line at identical weight; *his flatness is the joke, not a punchline
> read differently.*
> He also handles the can't-be-lost property in his own register without applying
> it to the player — no change needed.

## ART — The Nightmare
*false history: **you have been here before, every night** · technique: **the kindness you cannot refuse***

**ARRIVAL**
1. Dark. Good. You found the dark.
2. She was starting to worry you'd forgotten the way.
3. Forgotten, the way you always forget, and always find again.
4. You have been here before. Here, every night.
5. You never saw her in the light. You will not see her now either. That is alright.
6. She has been close. Close, and quiet, and near.

**DEMAND**
1. You have been carrying so much. So much, for so long.
2. Let her carry it instead.
3. **Put it down. Just for tonight. Just like every other night.**

**OPTIONS** — `Close your eyes.` / `Keep them open.`

**ON ACCEPTANCE**
1. There. There now.
2. She'll hold what you were carrying. She'll hold all of it.
3. Sleep light. She has you. She has always had you.

**ON REFUSAL**
1. She is not going anywhere. She was never going anywhere.
2. Sleep comes for everyone eventually.
3. She'll be here. Same dark, same her, every night.

**THE GIFT** *(enchanter's sword)*
1. Take this, so she won't have to worry. So you won't have to be afraid out there, and can always find your way back to her.
2. It will not break. It will not stay lost.

> ⭐ **Third person throughout**, alone among the six — derived from Ethan's own
> canon line, which already does it.
> **The gaslight lives inside the demand**: *"Just like every other night."* No
> separate assertion needed.
> **Best options in the set** — both physical acts, both about the blindness the
> player is actually sitting in, neither a yes or a no.
> The gift is a leash dressed as care: *"so you can always find your way back to
> her."* She does not think of it as a weapon.
> Agent idea worth taking: if the player has already found one of her pages, swap
> arrival line 5 for one that references it — *"You have always slept better than
> you think. You told her that."* Conditional dialogue that deepens the false
> history.

## Curation summary

| | change | why |
|---|---|---|
| ✅ | Blade: `Run.` → `Go.` | canon-reserved for the beat before a real Harvest |
| ✅ | Salvage: cut the third *friend* | the brief capped it at two so it lands |
| ✅ | Forge + Art: dropped the "and neither will you" clause | three of six converged; keep Wall's |
| ✅ | Forge's commercial vocabulary | over the cap but ancient, not corporate — leave |

**RESOLVED — refusing costs the patron.** The concern below stood: the player could
re-run the command immediately, which made six carefully-built refusal branches
decorative. Ethan's answer was that the patron **walks away**, and coming back too
soon returns nothing but absence. See **THE SILENCE** at the end of this file.

It also fits the asymmetry that was the worry in the first place — **four of six
patrons do not treat a refusal as final.** Wall asks again for less, Salvage circles,
Forge drags the chain past your door, Art simply waits; only Blade and Crown close
the door. A cooldown of silence lets all six stay in character: the four who never
leave are still out there, they are simply not answering yet.

# THE SILENCE — what a refused patron sounds like

*Rewritten 2026-08-13 on Ethan's correction. The first version characterised each
patron's absence; that was wrong, and the reason it was wrong is the whole design.*

## 🚨 ONE POOL. ALL SIX PATRONS. NOTHING IS THERE.

> Ethan: *"those patron reject lines should be used for all of them and it should
> not be like 'he is not coming' it should just be silence. ex 'The wind blows
> around you' 'You hear your own heartbeat' 'You take a breath'. It should be your
> actions."*

The six-pool version **broke its own premise.** If Blade's silence sounds like Blade
and Art's silence sounds like Art, then the patron is still present — still
performing, still characterised, still paying the player attention. Which means
refusing cost nothing. A characterised absence is just a quieter appearance.

The correction: **the lines are about YOU.** Your breath, your heartbeat, your
weight on the ground, the wind that was always going to blow whether you called
anybody or not. The player is returned to their own body in an ordinary world.

**Nothing in the pool may:**
* name or imply a patron — no *he*, no *she*, no *it*, no *something*
* imply being watched, waited for, or attended to in any way
* be supernatural, ominous, or atmospheric
* refer to the refusal, the cooldown, or the fact that a command was run

**The horror is that nothing happens.** Every one of these six characters has spent
a scene insisting the relationship was already underway and you were merely slow to
notice it. The punishment for declining is the world quietly demonstrating that this
was never true. Nobody is angry with you. Nobody is anywhere.

## The pool

Drawn at random. Grey italic — **never the red event text**, which throughout has
meant *something is happening*, and this is the deliberate absence of one.

1. You hear nothing but the wind.   ← **CANON, Ethan's line**
2. The wind blows around you.
3. You hear your own heartbeat.
4. You take a breath.
5. Your own breathing sounds loud to you.
6. You shift your weight. The ground creaks.
7. You lower your hands.
8. The air is cold on your face.
9. Somewhere far off, water is running.
10. You stand there a while longer.
11. You listen. The world makes its usual sounds.
12. Nothing happens.

## Notes

* **The canon-first rule is dropped.** It existed to make one recognisable line
  teach the rule before the flavoured pools started. With a single pool in a single
  register there are no flavoured pools to introduce, so the rule was scaffolding for
  a structure that no longer exists. Draw at random, canon line included.
* **`12. Nothing happens.`** is the bluntest line in the file and probably the best
  one. It is the only line that states the situation directly, and it can do that
  precisely because it credits nothing with the silence.
* ⚠️ **The one real risk: this is indistinguishable from a broken command.** A player
  who types the command and gets *"You take a breath."* may reasonably conclude
  nothing fired. Mitigations: the register is authored enough to read as intentional,
  it is always the same channel, and the player just refused that patron a few
  minutes ago. If it still plays as a bug in testing, the fix is **not** to explain
  the mechanic in the line — it is to make the refusal scene end more conclusively.
* Sound is the other lever, and better than words. `sound-physics-remastered` is
  installed; a single wind gust or a distant, ordinary ambient cue underneath the
  line would do more than any amount of text.
* **Retired:** the six characterised pools, ~26 lines. Kept in git history at
  `9ef1ac1` if the per-patron idea is ever wanted for something that is genuinely
  present.
