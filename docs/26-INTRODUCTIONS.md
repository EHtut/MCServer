# Introductions — the first time your patron speaks

*Six scenes, 2026-08-12, written by one Sonnet agent per patron and curated.
Mechanic: `23-THE-PATH-SYSTEM.md` §9.2 + Part IV (the attention ritual).
Death-ladder dialogue is `25-PATRON-DIALOGUE.md`.*

> **CANDIDATES, not canon.** The standing tone ruling is that the patrons' words
> are **Ethan's own writing**. Everything here is a draft for him to take, cut or
> rewrite.

## The shape

Ethan, 2026-08-12: *"Introductions should be the patron essentially introducing
themselves to you in a sort of almost obscure way? They then demand a sacrifice,
two choices. These shouldn't be actual losses just moral decisions. Like 'Take my
hand' Take hand - accept path, refuse - reject path."*

The screen goes black (blindness), the player is rooted (slowness) and made
invisible. Chat stays readable, so the world disappears and only the voice
remains. Then: **arrival → demand → two clickable options → accept or refuse.**

**The moral choice sits on top of a material one.** E2e already strips all XP on
entry — so the *decision* is moral (do you take the hand) and the *price of yes*
is everything you have. Every scene below alludes to that taking without once
explaining it.

## 🚨 Three things every agent found independently

**1. They all used the darkness diegetically.** None were told the black screen was
theirs to acknowledge. All six wrote to it anyway:

> *"Stand still. You have already begun to disappoint me."* — Blade
> *"Don't be afraid of the dark — I like to talk close."* — Salvage
> *"Be seated. You were never standing, but the correction costs nothing."* — Crown
> *"Dark. Good. You found the dark."* — Art

**2. THREE agents independently demanded staggered pacing** — one line at a time,
with real silence between, no crossfade. When three writers reach the same staging
note unprompted it is not a preference, it is the format telling you what it is.
**Implementation must space these lines seconds apart.** A wall of red text
arriving at once wastes the entire black screen.

**3. Sonnet, not Haiku.** The death ladder was one voice per beat and Haiku carried
it. This is a five-part scene in a fixed register and the difference is visible in
every file — most starkly on Forge, who produced MBA-speak on Haiku and
*"I was old before this dirt had a name"* on Sonnet.

## BLADE — The Challenger

**ARRIVAL**
1. Someone chose to look this way. Foolish.
2. I have knelt before nothing since the day I rose from it.
3. Hundreds have stood where you stand now. I remember none of their names.
4. Stand still. You have already begun to disappoint me.

**DEMAND**
1. A blade is worthless until it has failed once and cut anyway.
2. I do not want your devotion. I want to see if you are worth the trouble of watching.
3. My hand is out. Few live long enough to regret taking it.

**OPTIONS** — `Take his hand.` / `Let it fall.`

**ON ACCEPTANCE**
1. The gauntlet closes. Something old moves out of you and into him.
2. You feel lighter. That was never a mercy.
3. Good. Now you have nothing left to lose but yourself.

**ON REFUSAL**
1. Of course.
2. Better men than you have refused me. I have forgotten every one.
3. Go bleed on someone else's sword.

**ITEM — a worn whetstone**, black-veined, one edge already grooved down by use
that is not yours. It is not for sharpening. **It is for keeping count.** Every
trial wears it further, and only he decides when it is enough.

> ⭐ **The best item idea in the batch: the regard counter as a physical object.**
> The agent suggests it visibly wear across the first few trials, so his counting
> becomes diegetic. Deliberately anticlimactic refusal — no threat, no second
> offer. Do not give him a "final warning" line; contempt reads stronger as
> indifference.

## SALVAGE — The Hound

**ARRIVAL**
1. There you are. I wondered when you would wander this far.
2. I have watched hands reach for this life a long time before yours.
3. Most take more than they can carry, and pay for it in pieces.
4. Do not be afraid of the dark — I like to talk close.
5. Let us make a deal.

**DEMAND**
You have carried so much of yourself, alone, for so long, friend.
Let me hold some of it for you. I am good at holding things.
All I ask for is your hand.

**OPTIONS** — `Take her hand` / `Walk on alone`

**ON ACCEPTANCE**
There. That was not so hard, was it.
Everything you carried — I have it now, tucked in close, safe.

**ON REFUSAL**
Mm. Not tonight, then.
That is alright — I am not going anywhere. I never do.
I will still be circling.

**ITEM — a single brass cartridge**, warm as though just fired, though it has not
been. Not power: a taste of the one thing this path never stops needing, and a
promise she will always have more.

> ✂️ **CUT "Sleep well"** from her acceptance — that is Art's register leaking in,
> and these two must stay distinct. Refusal trimmed from four lines to three.
> *"Let me hold some of it for you"* is the XP strip reframed as a kindness with
> no lie in it anywhere, which is the best version of her trap.

## FORGE — The Thief

**ARRIVAL**
1. Every hand that ever built anything owed somebody first.
2. You did not invent that. Nobody does.
3. I was old before this dirt had a name.
4. Bells ring when a debt walks by. Mine never stop.
5. Sit still. This will not take long — unless you make it.

**DEMAND**
1. You will build. Furnaces, wheels, towers — I do not care what.
2. Whatever comes off your hands, I get first look, always.
3. Take my hand on it.

**OPTIONS** — `Take his hand.` / `Step back into the dark.`

**ON ACCEPTANCE**
1. There. See? Painless.
2. That is everything you had? Pathetic hoard.
3. No matter — there is plenty more coming.

**ON REFUSAL**
1. You made me wait for this?
2. Keep your hands empty, then. See how far that gets you.
3. I do not forget a wasted evening. We will speak again.

**ITEM — a single chain link**, still warm, torn from the length he drags. Not a
finished chain, and you both know he means to add more.

> **The reversal held.** Haiku drowned this character in "hemorrhaging
> productivity"; Sonnet gave him *"Bells ring when a debt walks by."* Being robbed
> and then **mocked for how little you had** is a far better opening than a threat.
> Agent suggests ambient bell/chain SFX under the blackness, and making the chain
> link a literal required component later so the debt keeps resurfacing.

## WALL — The Mother

**ARRIVAL**
1. Oh. You finally turned around.
2. I have been this close the whole time, love. You never noticed.
3. I do not mind. I am good at waiting.
4. Now that you are looking — there is no reason for anything to stand between us.

**DEMAND**
1. Let us start with something small between us.
2. Not much. Barely anything at all.
3. Give me your hand, love. That is the whole of it.

**OPTIONS** — `Take her hand` / `Keep your hand to yourself`

**ON ACCEPTANCE**
1. There. That is us, now.
2. Oh — I felt all of you, just then. Every bit of it.
3. I will keep it safe. We do not need anything else between us.

**ON REFUSAL**
1. You pulled your hand back. That is alright, love.
2. We will have plenty of time to try again.
3. **Next time I will ask for less. Just less than nothing, if that is easier.**

**ITEM — a single iron door hinge**, still warm. It cannot hang anything by
itself; a door needs two, and she has already decided which one she will bring.

> ⭐ **Held the hardest constraint in the batch: not one threat, anywhere.** She is
> not arriving — she is finally being *addressed*. The refusal is the most
> disturbing line written for this project: she does not accept a no, she
> **recalibrates downward**, and every later demand should be measurably smaller
> than this one so the escalation reads as compounding tenderness.
> `Keep your hand to yourself` is what you say to someone inappropriately
> familiar, which is exactly what she is and exactly what she will never
> understand about it.

## CROWN — The False King

**ARRIVAL**
1. Be seated. You were never standing, but the correction costs nothing.
2. A court has convened. You were simply not yet worth telling.
3. I have counted every hour you thought yourself unwatched.
4. Others call themselves protector, guardian, friend. I keep no such pretense.
5. You are here because something in you already answers to me.

**DEMAND**
I require no devotion. Devotion is for the desperate and the unclaimed.
Command in my name, and lesser things will die in yours.
Serve the court. It is a rank above what you currently hold.

**OPTIONS** — `I accept my summons.` / `I decline your court.`

**ON ACCEPTANCE**
Good. The account was always mine — I collect it now, in full.
What you carried, you no longer need to carry.
Rise. You are lighter now, and finally useful.

**ON REFUSAL**
Then I will not touch what is not offered.
Noted, and recorded — a rare line in a very long ledger.
The court remembers those who know their own mind. Go.

**ITEM — the Court Seal**, a signet ring of black iron stamped with a crown that
wears no face. Not a weapon: **a rank**. The first restless thing bound to it
kneels the instant it is worn, waiting to be sent somewhere its new master will
not have to go.

> **Line 4 does free worldbuilding.** *"Others call themselves protector, guardian,
> friend"* — Salvage literally calls you *friend*, Wall calls herself close. He is
> dismissing the other five patrons by manner, which implies **they know about each
> other.**
> **His refusal is the best-argued beat in the batch.** He is the only patron who
> respects a no, and the player comes away feeling they *gained* something by
> refusing — which is its own trap. Agent suggests resurfacing *"The court
> remembers those who know their own mind"* verbatim much later, as quiet proof he
> meant it.

## ART — The Nightmare

**ARRIVAL**
1. Dark. Good. You found the dark.
2. This is not new. You have been here before — every night — and called it nothing.
3. She has been close. Close, and quiet, and near.
4. You never saw her in the light. You will not see her now either. That is alright.
5. She is glad. She is glad you finally sat still long enough to talk.

**DEMAND**
1. You have been carrying so much. So much, for so long.
2. Let her carry it instead. Just for now. Just while you rest.
3. Close your eyes, and give her the weight.

**OPTIONS** — `Close your eyes.` / `Keep them open.`

**ON ACCEPTANCE**
1. There. There. Let her take that from you.
2. So heavy. You did not even notice how heavy.
3. Lighter now. Lighter, and hers to keep.

**ON REFUSAL**
1. Open, then. Open is alright too.
2. She is not going anywhere. She was never going anywhere.
3. Sleep comes for everyone eventually. She will be close when it does.

**ITEM — a single folded page**, ink still soft, pressed into your hand as your
eyes open. In a hand too even to be a person's:

> **"You have always slept better than you think."**

> ⭐ **SHE SPEAKS OF HERSELF IN THE THIRD PERSON**, alone among the six — and the
> agent derived that from Ethan's own canon line, which already does:
> *"She wants you to sleep. She wants you to sleep."* It makes her dissociated and
> dreamlike, as though the dark is describing her rather than her addressing you.
> **Best options in the batch** — both are physical acts, both are about the
> blindness the player is actually sitting in, and neither is a yes or a no.
> The page **sets the template for every page found later**: one warm,
> slightly-wrong line, never a hint and never an instruction.

## Open decisions

**1. Two patrons give half of something.** Wall's hinge needs a second she has
already chosen; Forge's chain link is one of many he means to add. Thematically
distinct — hers is intimacy, his is bondage — but it is the same *move*, and with
only two of six doing it they may read as variations on each other. Keep both,
or give one a whole object.

**2. Crown's options are first person** (`I accept my summons`) where the other
four are actions (`Take her hand`). Defensible as character — he is the only one
who expects an *answer* rather than an act — but it is the one inconsistency in
the set.

**3. Refusal outcome.** Mechanically all six should refuse the path. But three of
them do not treat it as final in the fiction: Wall will ask again for less,
Salvage is certain you will be back, Art simply waits. Only Crown and Blade treat
it as closed. **Does refusing set a cooldown, or can the player immediately
re-run the command?** Unresolved — and if it can be re-run instantly, the refusal
branch is beautiful writing with no weight behind it.

**4. Ambient SFX** under the black screen (Forge's bells and chains especially).
Would need a resource pack or existing sound events — unexplored.

# THE FLAGSHIP ITEMS

Ethan, 2026-08-12: *"these items should have enchantments, mending durability, and
they cannot leave your inventory... the idea is that these are sort of Flagship
items that each class needs to have and will keep on them. And when the path is
lost, they lose them."*

Not introductory trinkets and not high-tier trophies — **the signature tool of the
path**, carried permanently, taken away when the patron gives up on you.

| path | item | why it is the flagship |
|---|---|---|
| **forge** | `create:wrench` | ✅ Ethan. THE Create tool — rotate, dismantle, relocate. Never put down. |
| **blade** | `magistuarmory:steel_zweihander` | Epic Knights, steel tier — above bronze, below netherite. A two-hander for The Challenger. |
| **art** | `ars_nouveau:enchanters_sword` | ✅ Ethan. A tome was rejected because tomes get REPLACED as you upgrade; the sword is kept forever and merely **re-inscribed**. |
| **salvage** | `tacz:modern_kinetic_gun` + `GunId: rhino357` | ✅ Ethan wants a revolver. Three exist: `rhino357`, `taurus500`, `taurus943`. |
| **crown** | `goety:dark_wand` | The command interface for servants — the whole path runs through it. |
| **wall** | `securitycraft:universal_block_reinforcer_lvl1` | The one tool that defines the path. |

**Wall resolves the B1 tension rather than reopening it.** The reinforcer was made
deep-salvage-only to fix the dead path; as a *flagship* the walker is handed one and
**loses it on the fall**, while deep salvage stays as how anybody else finds one —
or how a fallen walker claws back a spare during their cooldown.

## Binding: RESTORATION, not prevention

**Nothing in the pack can bind an item to a player.** Every enchantment in every jar
was checked — no soulbound, no binding, nothing. So it must be scripted, and the
robust form is not intercepting drops, chest inserts, item frames, lava and death in
turn. It is simply:

> **If you walk a path and do not have your flagship, it comes back.**

Ethan, refining it: *"maybe instead it returns to your inventory if lost after a day
instead."* — **AFTER ONE IN-GAME DAY**, not instantly. Losing it should sting for a
session, and having the patron hand it back is far more characterful than a lock
that never let it go.

**The fall simply stops restoring it.** No confiscation code needed beyond a flag.

## Open

* ⚠️ **TaCZ guns may not accept vanilla Mending/Unbreaking** — they use their own
  durability model. Verify before promising the revolver never breaks.
* ⚠️ **AMMO SUPPLY.** Ethan: *"for salvage add all the ammo to their drops... idk if
  its possible to scale per equipped gun though."*
  * Our deep-salvage injectors currently supply only **7.62×39, 5.56×45, 12g**.
  * A revolver takes none of them, so as it stands she hands you a gun you can
    never feed.
  * Only **4** gun→ammo pairs could be extracted (`9mm`, `45acp`, `50ae`, `12g`);
    the rest of the 54 guns keep their ammo somewhere else in the gun-pack
    structure. **The full map needs another extraction pass.**
* ⚠️ **Per-gun ammo scaling is PLAUSIBLE BUT UNPROVEN.** TaCZ guns are one item
  (`tacz:modern_kinetic_gun`) carrying `GunId` in `minecraft:custom_data` — our own
  loot tables write exactly that. Reading it back means reading item components from
  KubeJS, which has never been tested here. **This needs an E0-style probe before
  anything is designed on it** — that is the whole reason E0 exists, and it has
  already caught two shipped defects today.
