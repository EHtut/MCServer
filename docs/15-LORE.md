# Veldora — the world of Cogs & Cadavers

**Status: DRAFT 2. Core canon set by Ethan 2026-08-03; the rest is scaffolding
around it and still argued with.** Sections marked **⟡ OPEN** are gaps.

Ethan's canon, verbatim, because everything below is built on it:

> The angels watch the descent. They watch the adventurers get closer. The
> adventurers cannot die, but they can be broken. Over and over and over.
>
> [The stalkers] represent the first cracks in this world. The Angels broken
> down from heaven to stalk the world.
>
> [The Vaults were] the old ones, the ones who took to the stars and when
> reaching, it shattered.

The rule this document follows: **every mechanic should have a reason in here,
and no reason in here should require a mechanic we cannot build.** Lore that
contradicts the config is worse than none, because players trust it and then get
punished for trusting it.

## 0. ⭐ THE FIVE GODS AND THEIR CHAMPIONS

*Ethan, 2026-08-15:*

> **This world is a cursed one, ruled over by the five gods. Each player, a champion.
> There have been many champions, you are not the first and you will never be the
> last.**

**Five gods, five paths.** The arithmetic works because Crown merged into Wall
(`35` §6). "Patron" was the role, **god** is the station, **champion** is what a player
is — and the third clause is the one that does the work: *you are not the first and
will never be the last* makes every player replaceable to the thing that chose them,
which is what the Harvest has always meant.

| god | station | path |
|---|---|---|
| **The Warrior** | god of war — *the absolute glory of combat* | blade |
| **The Hound** | the dog — *appetite, the favour that is a debt* | salvage |
| **The Thief** | the engineer's god — *never satisfied* | forge |
| **The Mother** | the spider — *closer, and closer* | wall |
| **The Nightmare** | ⭐ **the leader of the pantheon** — her realm is **magic** | art |

Each god's full project lives in its own doc, **`40-BLADE-THE-WARRIOR.md`** first.

⭐ **Art leads them, and is never present.** She works *through the shadow stalker* —
`nightmare_stalker` is her instrument, not her body. The god the others answer to is
the one no champion ever meets, and the god of war, who *"has knelt before nothing
since the day I rose from it"*, defers to her without being asked.

⚠️ **The gods are not a pantheon that agrees.** They hold stances on each other, and
those stances judge a god's *champions* separately from the god. Blade despises the
spider but respects the dog's champion; he demands cooperation with the engineer
because *glory cannot be attained alone.* That disagreement is content — `28` §4's
ambient arguments were the first sketch of it.

## 0b. ⏸️ THE DEPTHS — the goddess of death, and the last voice beside her

*Ethan, 2026-08-15. **Captured for later — "we increase the lore for the depths."**
Not scheduled; the lore gets written once everyone is set up.*

**The undead in the deep are servants of the goddess of death.** As you descend, a
voice speaks to you. Whispering. Raging.

> *"Stop. Leave."*
> *"She didn't want this, my goddess. They forced her hand."*
> *"They called us monsters, undead. We were a family."*
> *"I'm all that is left by her side, the last of her falsehoods."*
> *"The church tore us apart, and those gods you worship? They took what was left."*
> *"Just leave. Please."*

### 🚨 What this does to the world

**It implicates the five.** *"Those gods you worship? They took what was left."* The
pantheon a champion serves is not innocent of whatever happened down there — so the
deeper a champion goes, the more the voice tells them their own god is complicit.

That is a much sharper reason to descend than loot, and it gives the strata a moral
gradient the depth loop never had: **the lower you go, the worse your patron looks.**

* The voice **begs**, and it is the only thing in the world that does. Every god
  demands. This one says *please*.
* *"We were a family"* rhymes with Wall's household of the living and the dead —
  worth deciding whether that is coincidence or connection.
* **There is a church.** New, and it is neither the gods nor the champions.

### 🔑 It is already in Blade's mouth

His own contextual line for the deep, written by Ethan before this note:

> *"The realms of death. Delve deeper, for we must slay the enemies of that fel
> goddess."*

**The god of war already calls her fel and already sends you down to kill.** Whether
he is sending you against *her* enemies or against *her servants* is ambiguous in the
line as written — and that ambiguity is worth resolving deliberately, because one
reading makes him her ally and the other makes him her executioner.

## 1. Veldora

The world is old and magical and always has been. Dragons in the high places.
Things in the deep water. Herds that shake the ground. Ordinary people farm
turnips forty miles from something that could eat the village, and consider this
normal, because it is.

This is important and easy to lose: **Veldora is not a ruined world.** It is a
living one, and it is *loud*, and most of what can kill you here has simply
always been able to.

Then came **the old ones**.

They were not from elsewhere. That is the part people get wrong. They were of
Veldora — cleverer than anyone before or since, and they wanted **up**.

So they dug **down**.

That is the thing scholars cannot make peace with. To reach the stars the old
ones went into the earth, deeper every generation, building something enormous
and downward whose purpose was to throw them upward. Every layer of the Deep
Works is a course in a foundation. The bottom of the world is the base of a
ladder.

And they reached.

> And when reaching, it shattered.

What shattered is not agreed. The most common reading is the plainest one: they
touched the vault of heaven and it *broke*, the way a held thing breaks — not
destroyed, **cracked**.

Every account of what came next is missing, and they are all missing the same
part, which is worse than none of them having it.

---

## 2. The Angels

Something on the other side of that vault has been paying attention ever since.

The angels are not a religion here. There are no temples. Nobody prays to them,
because everybody understands, in the wordless way a herd understands a predator,
that they are not *for* us.

**They watch the descent.** That is all anyone can say with confidence. When you
go down, you are watched, and the watching gets heavier the deeper you go —
because down is where the reaching happened, and down is where it could happen
again.

The two facts every expedition is briefed on:

**You cannot die.** Not "should not". Cannot. Adventurers who fall get up. This
is not a blessing and it was not asked for. It is a condition of being watched by
something that has decided the matter is not finished with you.

**You can be broken.** Over and over and over. Nothing is taken from you
permanently except what you were carrying and what you were. Every expedition
that ends badly ends the same way — you wake up, you are whole, and you are a
little less than you were before, and there is no floor to that.

> The mercy and the sentence are the same clause. — a Deepworks chaplain,
> refusing to elaborate

**⟡ OPEN — what the angels want.** Deliberately blank. They watch; they do not
intervene; they permit no exit. Whether that is guardianship, containment, or
something with no human word is the biggest remaining hole, and it is the one a
dungeon master should eventually get to fill.

## 3. The first cracks — the stalkers

When the vault broke, some of them came through.

They did not arrive. They **fell**, and falling did to them what falling does.
What reaches Veldora is not an angel; it is what is left of one after the
descent. They do not command. They do not announce. They have no message.

They are the first cracks — not a threat that appeared later, but the earliest
visible symptom that the world is no longer sealed.

They are **layered**, and the layering is legible to anyone who survives enough
of them: *the deeper you find one, the less of it came through intact.*

| depth | what fell through |
|---|---|
| the Old Diggings | still nearly whole. Watches. Follows. Copies. Does not touch you for a long time. |
| the Deep Works | the shape is wrong now. It mimics, badly, and does not know that it is bad at it. |
| the Sealed Floor | nothing recognisable is left. It does not watch you. It is simply *between you and the way out.* |

**The Attendant** (*the Obsessed*) — takes an interest in one person. Follows,
copies, leaves things, learns what you say and says it back slightly wrong. The
attention arrives long before the violence, and people who have survived it
describe the attention as the worse half.

**The Knocker** — miners' lore older than the shafts it haunts. Three knocks
meant bad air, and crews that heeded it lived. Somewhere the knocking stopped
warning people and started *leading* them, and nobody can say when the meaning
turned over.

**The Voice** (*Revervox*) — repeats what it hears. It has no interest in you
until you make noise, and then it has a great deal.

*(Mechanically: the depth tiers in `config/incontrol/spawner.json` already do
this — vanilla mobs shallow, the horror roster entering at -1..-60, the worst of
it below -61. The fiction is describing the config, not asking for a new one.)*

## 4. The strata

Four bands. Miners use them, because getting them wrong kills you.

### The Turf — *sky down to about sixty*
Veldora alive and unbothered. Herds, predators, weather, things with teeth that
have always had teeth.

**The surface is dangerous, and it is honestly dangerous.** Nothing up here is
unnatural. A thing that eats you up here eats you because it is an animal and you
are meat, and it can be learned, avoided, tracked, outrun, and killed. That is
the whole contract of the surface: *the danger is real and the danger is fair.*

No fallen thing hunts the open fields. They cannot bear the sky — that is the
common explanation and it is at least consistent with observation.

### The Old Diggings — *sixty down to nothing*
Ordinary work by ordinary people. Cellars, shafts, props, the occasional
collapsed village that got greedy. Iron, copper, coal. People alive today have
grandfathers who cut some of it. Nothing here is a secret.

### The Deep Works — *nothing down to minus sixty*
Where the straight lines start, and where the ladder begins.

Corners become square. Shafts run the wrong way for ore, sized for something that
was not a person or was a person shaped wrong. The stone changes character. The
first of the metal that will not rust.

The watching starts here. Everyone notices; nobody agrees on when.

### The Sealed Floor — *minus sixty to the bottom*
Reinforced — not sturdy, *reinforced*, by people who expected to be attacked.

The black residue is here. It is not a mineral and does not occur; it is left
over, found only where they made it, in seams that follow no geology and
occasionally follow something that was obviously a floor plan.

The Vaults are here. So is the base of the ladder.

## 5. What can be made, and what cannot

**Nobody can make one of the old weapons.** Not a law, not a taboo — three ages
have had a serious go and each produced something that did nothing or killed the
smith.

A *cartridge*, though, can be made.

> A cartridge is a small cruelty. A pinch of the black residue, a cup of soft
> metal, a stone to throw, and a shape you can copy off a spent one with
> calipers. Any competent smith learns it in a season.
>
> A rifle is not a tool. A rifle is an argument between forty parts, and every
> part is the answer to a question we no longer know how to ask. We can feed
> them. We cannot father them.

So the trade is fixed: **you find the weapon, then you spend the rest of your
life feeding it.** A rifle is not a purchase, it is a dependency, and what it
depends on is at the bottom of the world — under the watching.

## 6. The Vaults

Reinforced shells at the base of the ladder. Doors barred from the inside.

The old ones built them *before* the reaching, not after. That detail matters:
they were not a response to disaster. They were provisioned in advance, by people
clever enough to reach heaven and clear-eyed enough to think it might not go
well.

What is in them is a weapon and a room's worth of the metal that does not break —
precisely the two things you need to survive what came through the crack.

They were not saving relics. They were leaving a **resupply**, for whoever was
still here afterwards.

Which is you.

**⟡ OPEN — the doors.** Keycard or explosive. What matters for the fiction is
whether opening one is robbing a tomb or *answering a message*, and the section
above quietly argues for the second.

## 7. Descending weakens the seal ✅

Ethan's ruling, and it closes the loop viciously.

Every descent is watched. Every descent is a step back toward the place where the
vault was broken. The world does not punish you for going down — it *notices*,
and what notices is on the other side of a crack that reaching made.

```
  you descend for the resupply
        ↓
  the descent is watched; the crack widens
        ↓
  more of them fall through
        ↓
  what falls through comes for what you built
        ↓
  surviving that needs the metal that does not break
        ↓
  which is at the bottom
```

**Getting stronger is what brings it.** There is no version of preparing that is
not also provoking. That is the shape of the game and it should never be softened
into a difficulty slider.

*(Mechanically: escalation already rides day count. Tying it to depth ACTIVITY —
how deep, how often, how recently — is the better fit and is buildable from the
telemetry that already records depth per player.)*

## 8. Where the players sit

Cogs and cadavers. The players are the cogs.

They are the current age, building in wood and brass and steam because that is
what this age can do — genuinely clever, and *visibly cruder than what is under
their feet.* That is the joke Veldora tells and never explains.

The surface industry funds the descent; the descent brings back what the surface
cannot make. Neither half works alone. A group that only builds gets bored. A
group that only dives gets broken.

**⟡ OPEN — is there a bottom?** Does the base of the ladder have an answer on it,
or is this a world where you get strong and the question stays shut? Only one of
those wants a final Vault.

## 10. Rules for anything added later

- **Never contradict the config.** If the fiction says the deep is lethal, the
  spawn tables must agree.
- **Explain restrictions, never apologise for them.** Every gate should read as a
  fact about the world, not an administrator's decision.
- **Leave the beginning missing.** The torn page is load-bearing. A world with a
  known origin is finished; one with a hole can be added to forever.
- **The surface is fair; the deep is not.** That is the real contrast — not safe
  versus dangerous. Up here things kill you for reasons. Down there they do not
  need one.
